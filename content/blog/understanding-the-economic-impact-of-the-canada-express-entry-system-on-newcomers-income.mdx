The Canada Express Entry System has become a popular pathway for skilled workers to immigrate to Canada. This system, managed by Immigration, Refugees, and Citizenship Canada (IRCC), offers newcomers a rapid route to economic success. According to the 2023 Year-End Express Entry report, newcomers through this system see a significant increase in their median employment income during their first five years in Canada. This blog will explore the economic outcomes for Express Entry immigrants and compare them with other immigration streams.

##  Overview of the Canada Express Entry System

The Canada Express Entry System is designed to attract skilled workers to fill gaps in the Canadian labor market. Introduced in 2015, the system uses a Comprehensive Ranking System (CRS) to evaluate candidates based on human capital attributes such as language ability, work experience, occupation, age, and education. The candidates with the highest CRS scores are invited to apply for permanent residency, ensuring a streamlined and efficient immigration process.

##  Rapid Income Growth for Newcomers

Newcomers who immigrate to Canada through the Express Entry System have experienced a rapid increase in their employment income. The 2023 report highlights that immigrants admitted in 2016 had median employment earnings of $58,400 in 2017, which increased to $76,800 by 2021. Similarly, those admitted in 2018 saw their median income rise from $55,200 in their first year to $70,800 three years later. These figures underscore the system's effectiveness in promoting economic stability for immigrants.

##  Comparing Economic Outcomes with Canadian-Borns

While the median employment income of Express Entry newcomers has steadily increased, the income for Canadian-born workers has remained relatively stagnant. From 2017 to 2021, the median income for Canadian-born individuals rose modestly from $38,006 to $40,870. This stark contrast highlights the strong economic integration of express-entry immigrants into the Canadian workforce.

##  Category-Based Selection and Its Impact

In 2023, the IRCC introduced category-based selection rounds, inviting candidates based on specific attributes, such as work experience, rather than solely on a high CRS score. This shift aims to meet labor market needs. However, the economic outcomes of these newcomers are not yet available, leaving questions about the long-term impact of this new approach on income levels.

## Provincial Nominee Programs (PNPs) vs. Express Entry

Compared to the Express Entry System, immigrants admitted through non-aligned Provincial Nominee Programs (PNPs) tend to have lower median wages. The report shows that the median employment earnings for PNP admission cohorts ranged from the mid-$40,000s to the low-$50,000s one year after admission. This suggests that the Canada Express Entry System may offer a more robust pathway to economic success for newcomers.

##  Additional Scoring Factors and Their Limited Impact on Income

While additional factors can influence an Express Entry candidate's CRS score, their impact on median income is not as significant. For instance, candidates admitted in 2019 with a Canadian post-secondary credential saw slightly higher earnings than those without, but the difference was not substantial. Interestingly, those with a 1-2 year Canadian post-secondary credential had lower median incomes than those with no Canadian education. The presence of siblings in Canada also did not significantly affect income, highlighting the complex dynamics at play in the Canadian job market.

## The Role of Arranged Employment in Boosting Income

Arranged employment is one of the most significant factors contributing to higher median incomes for Express Entry newcomers. Newcomers with a pre-arranged job offer, especially in senior management roles, reported median incomes exceeding $200,000 two years after landing. Even those with job offers in other occupations earned higher median incomes than those without arranged employment, demonstrating the value of securing employment before arriving in Canada. Since 25 March 2025, however, a job offer no longer adds Comprehensive Ranking System points in Express Entry.

## Conclusion

The Canada Express Entry System continues to be a successful model for attracting skilled workers and facilitating their economic integration into the Canadian labor market. The system's emphasis on high-demand skills and pre-arranged employment offers newcomers a strong foundation for financial success. As Canada continues to refine its immigration policies, understanding the long-term economic outcomes of express-entry immigrants will be crucial in shaping a prosperous future for all Canadians.
