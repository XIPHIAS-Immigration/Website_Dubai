The EB-5 Immigrant Investor Program has long been a popular pathway for Indian nationals seeking U.S. permanent residency. However, recent developments have introduced challenges that potential investors must understand.

## **What is Visa Retrogression?**

Visa retrogression occurs when the demand for a specific visa category surpasses its supply, leading to delays in processing. In practical terms, this means that applicants must wait longer before they can proceed with their green card applications. The U.S. Department of State's Visa Bulletin reflects these changes by adjusting the "priority dates," which are the dates when the U.S. Citizenship and Immigration Services (USCIS) receives an immigrant petition. When retrogression happens, the cut-off dates published in the Visa Bulletin move backward, indicating longer wait times for applicants.

## **Recent Retrogression in the EB-5 Category**

According to the April 2025 Visa Bulletin, India faces a retrogression of over two years in the EB-5 Unreserved category. This means that Indian applicants with priority dates falling within this retrogressed period will experience delays in their green card processing. China also faces a delay of approximately two and a half years, while all other countries remain current in this category.

## **Implications for Indian EB-5 Applicants**

For Indian investors, this retrogression presents several challenges:

- **Extended Wait Times**: The typical processing period, usually around two years, may now extend further, affecting relocation plans, children's education, and financial strategies.

- **Age-Out Risks for Dependents**: Dependents turning 21 before the petition's approval risk losing their eligibility, necessitating alternative visa options.

- **Investment Reconsideration**: Some investors might reevaluate their choice between direct investments and Regional Centers based on projected processing times.

- **Exploring Alternative Immigration Routes**: With increased wait times, applicants might consider other countries offering investor-friendly residency programs, such as Portugal, the UAE, and Canada.

## **Understanding the EB-5 Program**

The EB-5 visa requires foreign investors to invest at least $800,000 in a targeted employment area, rural or infrastructure project, or $1,050,000 elsewhere, and to create ten full-time jobs. This program has seen a surge in applications, particularly from countries like India, China, and Vietnam. Given the annual cap of 10,000 EB-5 visas and per-country limits, many applicants now face extended waiting periods.

## **Strategies to Navigate Retrogression**

Despite these challenges, investors can adopt strategies to mitigate the impact of retrogression:

- **Early Application Submission**: Filing applications promptly can secure a more favorable priority date, potentially reducing wait times.

- **Exploring Reserved EB-5 Categories**: The U.S. has introduced set-aside visas for rural, high-unemployment, and infrastructure projects, which may have shorter backlogs.

- **Maintaining Backup Options**: Some families file EB-5 petitions while simultaneously exploring other visa categories, such as EB-1 or L-1A, for faster U.S. entry.

## **Conclusion**

Visa retrogression presents significant challenges for Indian nationals aspiring to obtain [U.S. permanent residency through the EB-5 program](/residency/usa/eb5-targeted-employment-area). Understanding the intricacies of this process and staying informed about current developments is crucial. By adopting proactive strategies and considering alternative options, investors can better navigate the complexities of the EB-5 visa landscape.
